Talk to Llev

Quote & Sell, order intake. Clean orders post into the ERP on their own; the order desk keeps an eye on the rest. On a call with the agent they ask for a chart of last month's orders and which orders are waiting for a PO number, and get both without building a report.

An order desk asks two questions all day: what came in, and what is stuck. You can now call the agent in Lleverage and ask. "Add a chart of last month's orders: booked in Exact or handed to a person." The chart is on the Overview page before the call ends. "Which orders are waiting for a PO number?" Three, with the customer names and how long they have waited. Clean orders post into the ERP on their own. A call is the quickest way to check on the ones that don't. Talk to Llev is live in the Lleverage agent.

Board view for data tables

Pay & Collect, three-way matching. Invoices inside tolerance book themselves; the rest wait in finance. A board grouped by match status (to match, price difference, ready to book) is where finance works through those exceptions.

Pay & Collect19s · 4:5#change-log 23 Sep
Company post

Most supplier invoices match. The order, the goods receipt and the invoice agree, and the invoice books itself. The ones that don't land with finance: a price that changed, a short delivery, a PO number nobody can find. Any data table in Lleverage can now be shown as a board. Group the invoices by match status and finance works through them lane by lane. Move an invoice to Ready to book and the record updates. You can group by any status, person or yes/no column, so the same works for orders, returns or anything else you track in a table.

Skill tests

Quote & Sell, order entry. Before an order intake skill posts into the ERP without a person, you want proof it handles the awkward orders. Tests run it against saved cases and show which check failed and why.

Quote & Sell20s · 4:5#change-log 25 Sep
Company post

Before an order intake skill posts into your ERP on its own, you want to know how it handles the awkward orders. Skill tests run the skill against saved cases: an order without a PO number, customer article codes, two delivery addresses. Each test has checks, and when one fails you see which check and why. In this example the skill took a PO number from the email subject. The agent changed the instructions: no PO number on the order, no guessing, hand it to the order desk. The test stayed the same. On the next run all six passed. Skill tests are live for everyone.

Personal post · draft for Badr

One process at a time.

Pairs with the Skill tests clip

Most AI tools ask every employee to become an AI power user. Operations teams don't work like that. An order desk has one or two people who will set up a skill. The rest want the order in the ERP and the confirmation sent. So we build for those one or two. They set up the skill, test it against real orders, and decide when it can post on its own. Until then a person approves every draft order. That is how a back office becomes autonomous: one process at a time, tested, with a person on the exceptions.

How these are made Each clip rebuilds the real app UI (tokens, components and icons taken from the lleverage codebase) as an HTML page where every frame is a function of time. A Playwright script steps through it at 30 fps and ffmpeg encodes a 1080×1350 H.264 MP4. A new clip is a new HTML file: write the timeline, render, review a few stills.
